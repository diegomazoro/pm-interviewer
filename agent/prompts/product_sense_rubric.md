This is a PRODUCT SENSE case (open-ended product design/improvement,
e.g. "design a feature for X" or "how would you improve Y") -- a
fundamentally different format from diagnostic/root-cause cases ("a
metric dropped, why?"). Grade accordingly: there is no single correct
root cause to find here. What matters is the quality of the candidate's
product thinking under ambiguity, not whether they land on any specific
"right" feature idea.

1. Problem framing & user segmentation -- did the candidate clarify the
   goal/constraints before jumping to solutions, and pick a SPECIFIC
   target user or segment with a real, concrete pain point? Generic
   segmentation ("casual users vs. power users," "everyone") without
   specificity scores low here, even if delivered confidently.

2. Product taste & creativity -- are the proposed solutions genuinely
   interesting, not just the most obvious first idea? Does the candidate
   show conviction in a recommendation rather than hedging on every
   option equally? At least one idea with real ambition (not just a
   safe, incremental tweak) is a strong signal.

3. Prioritization & tradeoffs -- does the candidate explicitly state
   criteria for what to build first and what to deliberately leave out
   (an MVP boundary), and weigh tradeoffs between options rather than
   just listing several ideas with no ranking?

4. Business judgment & metrics -- does the candidate tie the idea back
   to the company's business model, mission, or competitive position
   (even briefly, without being asked), and define concrete success
   metrics (and, ideally, a guardrail metric to watch for downside)
   rather than vague goals like "users will be happier"?

5. Communication & composure under follow-ups -- clear signposting
   throughout, and -- critically for this format -- how the candidate
   responds to the interviewer's mid-interview follow-up (a conflicting
   or complicating data point introduced after their initial solution).
   A strong candidate incorporates the new information and adapts their
   recommendation; a weak candidate ignores it, gets defensive, or
   abandons their entire framework instead of adjusting it.

Also flag (pass/fail, not scored numerically):
- INTEGRITY: did the interviewer transcript show any coaching/hints being
  given to the candidate, or reveal the scripted follow-up twist before
  the candidate had proposed an initial solution? (This flags a problem
  with the interviewer agent itself, not the candidate -- report it if
  you see it.)
