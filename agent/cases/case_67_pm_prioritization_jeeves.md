# Case File 67 — Jeeves: Becoming the Main Spend Platform (PM Prioritization Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM at Jeeves. Next half you can build one of three things: an integration with the accounting software most Latin American SMBs use, a feature to pay suppliers' invoices from Jeeves, or business travel booking. Each has supporters on the team. Your Head of Product wants a recommendation. How would you decide?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Choose the investment that best serves the goal using data on customer needs and revenue.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal?" | Become customers' main spend platform -- measured by share of a customer's total business spend that goes through Jeeves. | On request -- key framing fact |
| 2 | "How much effort does each take?" | All three are roughly one half-year of work. | On request |
| 3 | "Where does customers' spend go today?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about customers' spend mix. | On request |
| 4 | "Why do customers stop using Jeeves or use it less?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about churn or reasons. | On request |
| 5 | "How does Jeeves make money on each?" | Card transactions earn about 1.8%; supplier payments would earn about 0.5% plus subscription upgrades; travel bookings earn commissions of about 4% of booking value. | On request |
| 6 | "What do competitors offer?" | Most competitors offer accounting integrations; some offer supplier payments; few offer travel. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate which option to choose -- let them weigh spend share, revenue, and retention.

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Typical Customer's Monthly Business Spend (excluding payroll)**

| Category | Share of spend | How it's paid today |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier invoices | 60% | Bank transfers |
| Software and ads | 20% | Mostly Jeeves cards |
| Travel | 8% | Mixed |

Average customer: about $100,000 of business spend a month.

| Reason | Share |
|---|---|
| "Reconciliation with our accounting takes too long" | 41% |

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Anchor on the goal: share of total spend through Jeeves.
2. Find where the spend is and why customers leave.
3. Estimate each option's effect on spend share, revenue, and retention.
4. Recommend, possibly sequencing.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Supplier invoices are 60% of spend: on $100K, moving even half = $30K a month x 0.5% = $150 a month per customer, plus a big jump in spend share.
- Travel: 8% x $100K = $8K x 4% = about $320 a month if fully captured -- higher revenue per dollar, but small share of spend.
- Accounting integration: doesn't add spend directly, but addresses the top churn reason (41%) -- protecting existing card revenue.

**Correct core insight:** The biggest lever for the goal is supplier payments: it's 60% of spend, currently outside Jeeves, and "we still need our bank" is the second churn reason. But the top churn reason is reconciliation with accounting -- and supplier payments without accounting integration would make reconciliation worse. A strong candidate either picks accounting integration (retention foundation, then supplier payments next) or supplier payments with a basic accounting export, explicitly reasoning about the dependency. Travel has nice margins but small share and doesn't address churn.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- A clear pick with reasoning, addressing both spend share and churn.
- A sequence (e.g. accounting integration now, supplier payments next half) or a scoped combination.
- Metrics: share of spend through Jeeves, reconciliation time, churn, revenue per customer.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: anchors on spend share, uses spend mix and churn data, quantifies, and handles the dependency between payments and accounting.
- 3/5: picks supplier payments or accounting with some data but misses the dependency or churn angle.
- 1/5: picks travel for its commission rate, or picks without data.
