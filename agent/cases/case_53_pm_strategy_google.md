# Case File 53 — Google: Growing Revenue from Local Businesses on Maps (PM Strategy & Growth Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Google Maps' business team. Millions of local businesses -- restaurants, salons, repair shops -- have free business listings on Maps. Leadership wants to grow revenue from these businesses and is debating two paths: showing more local ads on Maps, or launching a paid subscription for businesses with premium tools. They want your recommendation. How would you approach this?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Recommend how Maps should grow revenue from local businesses, weighing ads vs. subscriptions, user experience, and business value.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal?" | Grow revenue from local businesses without hurting the user experience that makes Maps valuable. | On request |
| 2 | "How many businesses and how many advertise today?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about the business base or current advertising. | On request |
| 3 | "How do users react to more ads on Maps?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about user impact of ads. | On request |
| 4 | "What would businesses pay for in a subscription?" | Reveal Exhibit 3 once asked about willingness to pay or what businesses want. | On request |
| 5 | "What do businesses struggle with most?" | Responding to reviews, keeping hours and menus updated, taking bookings, and understanding whether Maps brings them customers. | On request |
| 6 | "Are there competitors?" | Review sites and booking platforms sell subscriptions and booking tools to local businesses. | On request |
| 7 | "Would a subscription affect ranking on Maps?" | It must not -- ranking has to stay based on relevance and quality to keep users' trust. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate which path to choose or how to combine them -- let them weigh revenue against user trust and business needs.

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Clarify the goal and constraint (user trust).
2. Size each path: more ads vs. subscriptions.
3. Weigh user impact and business needs.
4. Recommend a path or combination with metrics.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Current ad revenue: 2M x $1,200 = about $2.4B a year; +20% from more ads = about +$480M, but with fewer searches (-3%) and lower trust.
- Subscription: 48M non-advertisers x 18% = 8.6M x $25 x 12 = about $2.6B -- survey numbers overstate, so a realistic share (say a third) is still about $0.9B.
- 61% don't advertise because they can't see results -- a measurement problem worth solving.

**Correct core insight:** More ads produce revenue quickly but erode user trust and engagement, which is Maps' core asset. The bigger, healthier opportunity is helping businesses: a subscription with operational tools (bookings, review replies, insights) serves real needs and doesn't touch ranking. And proving ad results to the 61% who don't advertise could grow ad revenue without increasing ad load.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Don't increase ad load on Maps.
- Launch a business subscription focused on tools, with a firm rule that it doesn't affect ranking; test pricing and adoption.
- Improve ad measurement (e.g. visits and calls from ads) to convert more businesses to advertising.
- Metrics: subscription adoption and retention, advertisers count, user trust and searches per user as guardrails.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: sizes both paths with realistic adjustments, protects user trust, recognizes the measurement problem, and recommends a tools-based subscription plus better ad measurement.
- 3/5: reasonable choice but doesn't quantify or ignores trust.
- 1/5: recommends more ads for quick revenue or "sell better ranking."
