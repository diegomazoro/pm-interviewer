# Case File 63 — Amazon: Tools for Third-Party Sellers (PM Prioritization Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Amazon's seller tools team, which builds software for the independent businesses that sell on Amazon. Next half you can invest in one of three tools: automated pricing that adjusts prices to stay competitive, a simpler way to handle returns, or a tool that helps sellers list their products in other countries. Your director wants a recommendation. How would you decide?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Choose the seller tool that best grows the stated goal, considering reach, impact, and side effects.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal?" | Grow sales by independent sellers on Amazon (third-party sales) while keeping a good customer experience. | On request -- key framing fact |
| 2 | "How much effort does each take?" | All three are about one half-year of work. | On request |
| 3 | "Who would use each tool?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about reach or which sellers would use each. | On request |
| 4 | "What's the expected impact?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about impact or estimates. | On request |
| 5 | "Are there risks for customers?" | Automated pricing tools can create price wars that hurt seller margins; some sellers leave if margins collapse. | On request |
| 6 | "Do sellers already have options for these?" | Many large sellers already use outside repricing software. Few small sellers use any. International expansion tools exist but are complex. | On request |
| 7 | "Why do sellers struggle with returns?" | Small sellers spend a lot of time on returns and often lose money because they can't resell returned items. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate which tool to choose -- let them weigh reach, incremental impact, and risk.

---

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Reach**

| Tool | Sellers likely to use it | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Automated pricing | 30% of active sellers | Most large sellers already use outside tools |
| Simpler returns | 70% of active sellers | Benefits small sellers most |
| International listing | 8% of active sellers | Requires stock, shipping, and support in other countries |

**Exhibit 2 — Estimated Impact for Users of Each Tool**

| Tool | Expected effect on users' sales | Other effects |
|---|---|---|
| Automated pricing | +6% (mostly by taking sales from other sellers on the same product) | Lower prices for customers; margin pressure for sellers |
| Simpler returns | +3% (sellers can offer easier returns, which increases conversion) | Lower seller costs; fewer sellers leaving due to return losses |
| International listing | +25% | Highest complexity; slow to adopt |

---

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Anchor on the goal (third-party sales, good customer experience).
2. Estimate total impact: reach x effect, and whether it's incremental or shifted.
3. Consider side effects (price wars, margins, seller retention).
4. Recommend with metrics.

**Quantification a strong candidate does:**
- Pricing: 30% x 6% = about 1.8% of third-party sales -- but much of it shifts sales between sellers rather than creating new sales, so the true net gain is lower.
- Returns: 70% x 3% = about 2.1% -- largely incremental (higher conversion), plus lower costs and seller retention.
- International: 8% x 25% = 2% -- incremental, but slow adoption and high complexity.

**Correct core insight:** The three look similar at first glance (~2%), but the quality of the impact differs. Pricing mostly reshuffles sales between sellers and risks price wars; international is incremental but slow and limited to few sellers. Simpler returns has the widest reach, is incremental, and also reduces costs and seller churn -- especially for small sellers. A strong candidate distinguishes incremental from shifted sales.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Build simpler returns, with a focus on small sellers.
- International as a longer-term bet; pricing deprioritized given outside tools and price-war risk.
- Metrics: third-party sales, conversion on listings with easy returns, seller retention, seller costs per return.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: computes reach x effect, distinguishes incremental vs. shifted sales, weighs side effects, and recommends returns or a well-argued alternative.
- 3/5: computes impact but treats all gains as equal.
- 1/5: picks international because +25% is the biggest number.
