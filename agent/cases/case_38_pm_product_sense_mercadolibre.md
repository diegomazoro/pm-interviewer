# Case File 38 — Mercado Libre: Winning the First Purchase in Smaller Cities (PM Product Sense Case)

**Case type:** product_sense

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a Product Manager at Mercado Libre. In smaller cities and towns across Latin America, millions of people now have smartphones and internet access, but have never bought anything online. Many of them browse Mercado Libre but don't complete a first purchase. How would you design an experience that gets more of these people to make their first online purchase?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Design an approach that converts first-time online shoppers in smaller cities, with a clear target user, MVP, tradeoffs, and success metrics.

---

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate whether the main barrier is trust, payment, delivery, or digital skills, or which solution to choose -- they need to find and decide it.

**Solution-gated follow-up (deliver once, verbatim, only after the candidate proposes a concrete initial solution/direction -- not before):** "Interesting -- one thing that complicates this: in a pilot offering cash on delivery to first-time buyers in these cities, first purchases went up 30%, but 22% of those orders were refused at the door, and each refused order cost Mercado Libre about three times its shipping fee in return logistics."

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Strong problem framing:** identifies trust (fear of paying and not receiving) as the main barrier, and the checkout step as the biggest drop-off. Picks a specific segment, e.g. first-time buyers without a credit card in smaller cities.

**Product taste:** strong ideas include a "first purchase guarantee" with clear money-back messaging; paying at a local store after confirming the order; tracking with simple, frequent updates; pickup points at local stores; social proof from local buyers ("120 people in your town bought this"); easy, free returns for the first purchase; guided first purchase with an assistant. An ambitious option: a local agent network (store owners) who help people place their first order.

**Prioritization & tradeoffs:** MVP such as a first-purchase guarantee + cash payment at local stores + pickup points, leaving out a full agent network for v1. Discusses trust vs. cost (guarantees and returns cost money).

**Business judgment & metrics:** ties to new-buyer growth and lifetime value. Metrics: first-purchase conversion, second purchase within 60 days, refused/returned orders. Guardrail: cost per acquired buyer.

**Composure under the follow-up:** cash on delivery solves trust but creates costly refusals. A strong candidate adapts: limit cash on delivery to lower-risk orders, ask for a small prepayment, use pickup points where the buyer checks the item before paying, or use buyer history and phone verification -- and measures cost per successful first purchase, not just first purchases. A weak candidate drops the idea or ignores the cost.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5: uses the data to find trust as the barrier, specific segment, focused MVP, metrics including repeat purchase and cost, thoughtful adaptation.
- 3/5: reasonable ideas but doesn't use the research, or generic metrics.
- 1/5: proposes discounts or ads to bring traffic, missing the trust barrier.
