# Case File 41 — Mercado Libre: Responding with a Cross-Border Low-Cost Offer? (PM Strategy & Growth Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Mercado Libre's strategy team. Low-cost cross-border marketplaces that ship cheap goods directly from Asia are growing fast in Latin America, especially for low-priced items like accessories, gadgets, and fashion. Leadership is debating whether Mercado Libre should launch its own cross-border program with sellers shipping directly from Asia. They've asked for your recommendation. How would you approach this?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Recommend whether and how Mercado Libre should launch a cross-border program, weighing competitive threat, economics, delivery, and impact on local sellers.

---

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate whether to launch, which categories to target, or how to protect local sellers -- let them reason through it.

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Size the threat: where are competitors winning, and how much of Mercado Libre is exposed.
2. Assess Mercado Libre's right to win vs. cross-border players.
3. Evaluate the cross-border program's economics and risks (regulation, local sellers).
4. Consider alternatives (strengthen local low-cost offer).
5. Recommend a focused strategy.

**Correct core insight:** The threat is concentrated in low-priced orders (under $20, where competitors went from 6% to 19%) -- about 12% of Mercado Libre's GMV. Above $50, competitors barely exist, so the core business is safe. A full cross-border program has thin economics ($0.40 margin per order), slow delivery that undercuts Mercado Libre's main advantage, regulatory risk (import tax changes could raise prices 15-30%), and could anger local sellers. A targeted response fits better: a limited cross-border offering in categories where local supply can't compete on price, plus strengthening local low-cost supply where it can.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Exposure: 12% of GMV is in the contested segment; the rest is largely unaffected.
- Economics: $11 x 12% = $1.32 revenue per order, $0.40 contribution -- thin, and at risk if tax exemptions disappear.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Don't launch a broad cross-border marketplace; use a limited, curated program in specific categories with consolidated shipping, clearly labeled.
- Defend with local strengths: help local sellers source cheaper, promote fast delivery and easy returns for low-priced items.
- Plan for the regulatory scenario: if import exemptions end, cross-border competitors lose their price edge.
- Metrics: share of under-$20 orders, overlap buyers' share of wallet, local seller GMV, margin per order.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: identifies where the threat is concentrated, evaluates thin economics and regulatory risk, protects the local seller base, and recommends a targeted response.
- 3/5: reasonable recommendation but treats the threat as company-wide, or ignores regulation or local sellers.
- 1/5: recommends copying competitors fully, or ignoring them, without analysis.
