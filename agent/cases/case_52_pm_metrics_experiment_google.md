# Case File 52 — Google: More Shorts in the YouTube Home Feed? (PM Metrics & Experimentation Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on YouTube's home feed. Your team tested showing more Shorts -- short vertical videos -- in the home feed. In the test group, total watch time per user went up 5%. The Shorts team wants to roll it out to everyone. Your director asks you to make sure this is a good decision. How would you evaluate it?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Evaluate whether showing more Shorts is good for YouTube overall, beyond total watch time.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the home feed team's goal?" | Long-term viewer satisfaction and a healthy business for YouTube and creators. | On request |
| 2 | "How was the test run?" | Users were randomly split 50/50 for four weeks. | On request |
| 3 | "What happened to Shorts and long-form watch time separately?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked for a breakdown by format. | On request |
| 4 | "How does revenue differ between Shorts and long-form?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about revenue, ads, or monetization. | On request |
| 5 | "How were creators affected?" | Reveal Exhibit 3 once asked about creators. | On request |
| 6 | "What about viewer satisfaction?" | In-app surveys showed no significant change in satisfaction between groups. | On request |
| 7 | "Did the effect change over the four weeks?" | The total watch time lift was steady across all four weeks. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not point to cannibalization, revenue per minute, or creator effects -- the candidate should look beyond total watch time on their own.

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Break down the total: what grew and what shrank.
2. Translate into revenue and creator impact.
3. Check viewer satisfaction and long-term risks.
4. Decide, possibly with a more targeted approach.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Long-form: -9 minutes; Shorts: +15 minutes; total +6 minutes (+5%).
- Revenue per user per day: control 100 x $0.010 + 20 x $0.003 = $1.06; test 91 x $0.010 + 35 x $0.003 = $1.015 -> about -4%.
- Shorts are cannibalizing long-form watch time, which earns over three times more per minute.

**Correct core insight:** Total watch time rose 5%, but revenue per user fell about 4% because Shorts replaced higher-earning long-form viewing. Long-form creators lose 9% of views and earnings, which risks the supply of the content YouTube earns most from. Satisfaction didn't improve. "More watch time" here is not a win for the business or the ecosystem.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Don't roll out as is.
- Use a combined metric: revenue and satisfaction, and creator health, not total watch time alone.
- Explore targeting: more Shorts for users who mostly watch Shorts or in contexts where they don't replace long-form (e.g. short sessions), and test that.
- Longer-term: work on Shorts monetization; until it improves, cannibalization is costly.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 insight generation: breaks down watch time, computes revenue per user, flags creator impact, and recommends a targeted test with better metrics.
- 3/5: notices cannibalization but doesn't quantify revenue or creator effects.
- 1/5: recommends rollout because total watch time went up.
