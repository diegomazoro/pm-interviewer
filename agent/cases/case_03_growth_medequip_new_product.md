# Case File 3 — MedEquip Co New Product Launch (Growth Strategy Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"Our client is MedEquip Co, a manufacturer of hospital equipment with $500M in revenue, primarily selling patient monitoring systems to large hospitals. Their R&D team has developed a portable version of their flagship monitor, designed for home health care and small clinics — a new market for the company. The CEO wants to know: should MedEquip launch this new product line, and what would it take to succeed? How would you think about this?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Decide whether to launch the portable monitor into the home-health/small-clinic market, and outline what's needed to succeed.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Why is the CEO considering this now?" | Hospital equipment growth has slowed to ~2%/year; the CEO is looking for a new growth vector, and R&D already built a working prototype (sunk cost, not new investment ask). | On request |
| 2 | "What's the size and growth of the home health / small clinic monitoring market?" | ~$3B market, growing ~12%/year, driven by aging population and a shift of care from hospitals to home settings. | On request |
| 3 | "Who competes in this new market today?" | Two established players with ~60% combined share selling simpler, lower-cost monitors; a handful of smaller startups with app-connected devices targeting the same segment. | On request |
| 4 | "How does the new product compare to what's out there — price, features?" | The prototype has more clinical-grade accuracy (inherited from MedEquip's hospital-grade tech) but costs roughly 2x the typical product in this market ($800 vs. ~$400 average). | On request — important tension for candidate to surface |
| 5 | "Does MedEquip have a sales channel for this market already?" | No — MedEquip's sales force calls on hospital procurement departments; this new market is sold through home health agencies, durable medical equipment (DME) distributors, and increasingly direct-to-consumer online. | On request — key capability gap |
| 6 | "What would it cost to launch, and what capital is available?" | Estimated $15M to scale manufacturing, build a new sales/distribution channel, and pursue relevant regulatory clearance for the home-use setting (separate from the existing hospital-use clearance). | On request |
| 7 | "Is there a regulatory difference between hospital-use and home-use devices?" | Yes — home-use medical devices require separate regulatory clearance addressing use by non-clinical staff/patients, which the current product does not yet have. | Reveal if asked about regulatory/approval requirements |
| 8 | "What margins does MedEquip get in its core hospital business vs. what might be realistic here?" | Core hospital business: ~35% gross margin. New market's typical competitor margins: ~25%, given lower price points and channel/distributor cuts. | On request |
| 9 | "Would this cannibalize existing hospital sales?" | Minimal expected overlap — home health/small clinic buyers are largely not existing hospital customers, though some small clinics currently buy scaled-down versions of the hospital product directly from MedEquip at hospital pricing. | Reveal if asked about cannibalization — candidates should think to ask this |
| 10 | "Can I see exhibits / market data?" | Hand over Exhibit 1 once a framework is proposed. | Framework-gated |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate whether the price premium is justified or whether the channel gap is a dealbreaker — let them reason to a view using the facts above.

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Market attractiveness (size, growth, competitive intensity).
2. Product-market fit (does the product's positioning — price, features — match what this market actually values).
3. Capability gap analysis (channel, regulatory, manufacturing) and cost to close it.
4. Financial case (cost to launch vs. realistic revenue/margin capture) and cannibalization risk.

**Correct core insight:** The market is attractive on paper (large, fast-growing), but MedEquip's prototype is priced roughly 2x the market average in a segment where 70% of buyers say price is the top factor — meaning the product as currently specced is mismatched to the majority of the market and only clearly fits the ~30% high-acuity sub-segment willing to pay for clinical-grade accuracy. Compounding this, MedEquip has no existing channel into DME distributors/home health agencies (85% of the market buys through 2-3 major distributors), and lacks home-use regulatory clearance — both real, costed gaps ($15M, per Exhibit 2), not just theoretical risks.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- A segmented "yes, but" recommendation: target the high-acuity, accuracy-sensitive sub-segment first (roughly 30% of the market) rather than the whole market, where the price premium is defensible.
- A channel plan that explicitly addresses the distributor gap (e.g., partner with 1–2 existing DME distributors rather than building a direct sales force from scratch, given the $15M budget and speed considerations).
- Acknowledgment that regulatory clearance is a gating item on the critical path, not just a line-item cost.
- A note on cannibalization risk with existing small-clinic hospital-pricing customers, and a plan to manage channel conflict/pricing consistency.
- A view on whether $15M to launch is justified given the addressable (segmented) opportunity size — not just the full $3B market growing at 12%.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 insight generation: candidate identifies the price/segment mismatch unprompted, connects it to the survey data (Exhibit 3) if requested, and arrives at a segmented go-to-market recommendation rather than an all-or-nothing yes/no.
- 3/5: candidate notices the price is high but doesn't connect it to a segmentation strategy, or misses the channel-gap cost entirely.
- 1/5: candidate recommends a blanket "yes, launch" or "no, don't" without weighing price-segment fit, channel gap, or regulatory timing.
