# Case File 55 — Meta: WhatsApp Business Roadmap in Brazil (PM Prioritization Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on WhatsApp Business in Brazil, where millions of small businesses sell to customers through WhatsApp chats. Next half you can build one of three things: in-chat payments with a product catalog, an AI assistant that answers common customer questions automatically, or better tools for sending marketing messages to customers. Your director wants a recommendation. How would you decide?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Choose the initiative that best serves the goal, considering revenue, adoption, and customer experience.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal for WhatsApp Business in Brazil?" | Grow revenue from paid business messaging (businesses pay per marketing or service conversation they start), while keeping WhatsApp pleasant for regular users. | On request -- key framing fact |
| 2 | "How much effort does each take?" | All three are roughly one half-year for the team. | On request |
| 3 | "How do Brazilian customers pay small businesses today?" | Mostly with Pix, the instant payment system, by copying a key from the chat into their banking app. | On request |
| 4 | "What's the expected impact of each?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about impact or revenue. | On request |
| 5 | "What risks come with each?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about risks, user experience, or downsides. | On request |
| 6 | "What do businesses ask for?" | Small businesses most often ask for help answering the same questions repeatedly (prices, hours, availability) -- especially outside working hours. | On request |
| 7 | "Do users like receiving marketing messages?" | Some do, but users block or report businesses that message too often; Meta limits marketing messages per user. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate which project to pick or how to weigh revenue against user experience -- they need to make the call.

---

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Estimated Annual Impact (Brazil)**

| Initiative | Estimate |
|---|---|
| In-chat payments + catalog | No direct messaging revenue; could increase sales per business by ~10% and businesses' engagement with WhatsApp Business. Payments competes with Pix, which is free and already easy. |
| AI customer assistant | Businesses using it would start ~25% more service conversations, about +$60M in messaging revenue; also faster replies for customers. |
| Marketing message tools | About +$110M in messaging revenue if businesses send more campaigns. |

**Exhibit 2 — Risks**

| Initiative | Risk |
|---|---|
| In-chat payments | Low adoption risk: an earlier payments pilot reached only 4% of businesses because Pix was already convenient. |
| AI customer assistant | Wrong answers could annoy customers; needs good business data (catalog, hours). |
| Marketing tools | In a test, users receiving more marketing messages blocked businesses 2.5x more often, and reported 18% lower satisfaction with WhatsApp. |

---

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Anchor on the goal (messaging revenue) and the constraint (keep WhatsApp pleasant).
2. Compare revenue impact, adoption likelihood, and user-experience risk.
3. Recognize local context (Pix makes payments hard to win).
4. Recommend with guardrails.

**Correct core insight:** Marketing tools have the biggest revenue estimate ($110M), but they hurt the core user experience (blocks 2.5x, satisfaction -18%) -- which threatens WhatsApp's main asset and future revenue. Payments face a strong free local alternative (Pix) and a weak pilot (4%). The AI assistant ($60M) grows revenue while making the experience better for customers and addressing businesses' top request. A strong candidate picks the assistant, or picks marketing tools only with strict frequency limits -- but must address the user-experience risk explicitly.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Build the AI customer assistant, starting with common questions (prices, hours, availability) using business-provided data.
- Guardrails: answer accuracy, customer satisfaction, easy handoff to a human.
- Revisit marketing tools later with strict user-protection limits; deprioritize payments given Pix.
- Metrics: service conversations started, messaging revenue, reply time, block rate.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: weighs revenue against user-experience harm, uses the Pix context, picks a path that grows revenue without damaging the platform, with guardrails.
- 3/5: picks a reasonable option but ignores the user-experience risk or the Pix context.
- 1/5: picks marketing tools because the revenue number is largest, without addressing harm.
