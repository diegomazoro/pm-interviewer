# Case File 45 — Anthropic: Building Vertical Products or Staying a Platform? (PM Strategy & Growth Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Anthropic's strategy team. Today, many companies build specialized products on top of Claude -- for example, AI tools for lawyers, financial analysts, or doctors. Some leaders think Anthropic should build its own products for one or two of these industries to capture more value. Others worry it would put Anthropic in competition with its own customers. Leadership wants your recommendation. How would you approach this?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Recommend whether and where Anthropic should build vertical products, weighing value capture, channel conflict, and right to win.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal -- revenue, strategic position, something else?" | Grow revenue and strengthen Anthropic's position, without damaging the API business that most revenue currently depends on. | On request |
| 2 | "How much revenue comes from vertical customers on the API?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about revenue by industry or customer type. | On request |
| 3 | "How strong are the existing vertical products?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about the competitive landscape by industry. | On request |
| 4 | "What advantage would Anthropic have in a vertical?" | Direct access to the latest models and the ability to tune them, but no industry-specific data, sales teams, or customer relationships in these industries. | On request |
| 5 | "How would API customers react?" | In interviews, several large API customers said they would "seriously consider" moving some volume to other model providers if Anthropic competed with them directly. | On request -- key risk |
| 6 | "Are there alternatives to building full products?" | Yes -- Anthropic could build industry-specific features into its platform (e.g. evaluation tools, compliance features) or partner with leading companies in a vertical. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate whether to build, partner, or stay horizontal, or which industry to choose -- let them weigh value capture against channel conflict.

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Clarify the goal and the API dependency.
2. Assess channel conflict: how much revenue comes from customers in each vertical.
3. Assess right to win per vertical (data, relationships, competition).
4. Compare options: build, partner, platform features, stay horizontal.
5. Recommend a focused path with guardrails.

**Correct core insight:** Building in legal would directly compete with concentrated, well-entrenched customers who represent 9% of API revenue and could move volume elsewhere -- high conflict, low right to win. Healthcare has very high complexity and regulation. Financial analysis is fragmented with no clear leader, lower conflict per customer, and medium complexity -- the most plausible place to build, if anywhere. But the stronger default is to capture vertical value through the platform (industry-specific tools that help all builders) and partnerships, and only build a product where no strong partner exists.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Revenue at risk if legal customers move even half their volume: 9% x 50% = 4.5% of API revenue.
- Financial analysis exposure is lower and spread across many small customers.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Don't build in legal or healthcare now.
- Invest in vertical platform capabilities (industry evaluations, compliance, connectors) that make Claude the best model for every builder.
- If building, consider a focused financial analysis product, with clear communication to customers, or partner with a leader instead.
- Metrics: API revenue retention from vertical customers, share of vertical builders choosing Claude, revenue from any new product.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: quantifies channel-conflict risk, compares verticals on right to win, considers platform and partnership alternatives, and recommends a focused path.
- 3/5: recognizes channel conflict but doesn't compare verticals, or recommends a vertical without considering alternatives.
- 1/5: recommends building in the biggest vertical because the revenue is large, ignoring conflict.
