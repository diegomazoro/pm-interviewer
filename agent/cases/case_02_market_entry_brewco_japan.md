# Case File 2 — BrewCo Japan Entry (Market Entry Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"Our client is BrewCo, a U.S. craft beer company with $150M in annual revenue, strong in the domestic premium beer segment. Growth in the U.S. has slowed, and the CEO is considering entering Japan as the company's first international market. She's asked us: should BrewCo enter Japan, and if so, how? What's your approach?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Decide whether to enter Japan, and if yes, recommend an entry mode.

---

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |

If asked "which would you recommend," respond: "I'm interested in your view — talk me through the tradeoffs as you see them."

**Exhibit 2 — Entry Mode Cost/Control Tradeoffs (reveal only the row(s) relevant to what candidate proposes)**

| Entry mode | Approx. upfront cost | Speed to market | Control over brand/quality | Key risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Export via local distributor | $2–5M | Fast (12–18 months) | Low — distributor controls pricing/placement | Weak brand control, thin margins after distributor cut |
| Joint venture with local brewer | $10–15M | Medium (18–24 months) | Medium — shared decision-making | Partner alignment risk, profit sharing |
| Licensing / contract brewing locally | $5–8M | Medium (12–24 months) | Medium — quality control depends on contract terms | IP/recipe control risk, quality consistency |
| Acquire a small local craft brewer | $20–30M | Slower initially (24+ months to integrate) but fastest path to real local presence/distribution access | High | Full deployment of available capital, integration risk |

**Exhibit 3 (reveal only if asked about consumer specifics) — Craft Beer Consumer Profile in Japan**

- 65% of premium/craft buyers are under 40, concentrated in Tokyo/Osaka urban areas.
- Price sensitivity is lower in this segment; willing to pay a premium for perceived quality/authenticity/story.
- Retail channel split: ~40% specialty/liquor stores, ~35% convenience stores (very high-traffic in Japan), ~25% on-premise (bars/restaurants).

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Market attractiveness (size, growth, competitive intensity, entry barriers).
2. Company capability/fit (can BrewCo win here — brand, product, capital, supply chain).
3. Entry mode options and tradeoffs (self-generated, not fed by interviewer).
4. Financial/risk assessment of the recommended path.

**Correct core insight:** The total Japanese beer market is unattractive (flat, incumbent-dominated), but the premium/craft sub-segment is small yet fast-growing and matches BrewCo's actual positioning — the candidate needs to correctly narrow scope to that sub-segment rather than evaluating "the Japanese beer market" as a monolith. Given no existing brand presence, no portable supply chain, and Japan's distribution-license realities, a pure export/distributor deal is fast but leaves BrewCo with little control and thin economics, while acquiring a small local craft brewer solves the distribution-access and license problem simultaneously and buys instant local credibility, at the cost of most of the $30M budget and integration risk. A joint venture is a reasonable middle path a strong candidate might land on instead, provided they explicitly weigh the tradeoff versus acquisition.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Explicit segmentation: enter via the craft/premium segment only, not mass-market.
- A clearly reasoned entry-mode choice (any of JV, licensing, or acquisition can be "correct" if well-argued against the $30M capital constraint and control needs) — export-only is a weak final recommendation because it doesn't solve the brand-building problem.
- Acknowledgment of the distribution/licensing barrier as a reason a local partner (in some form) is close to mandatory.
- A stated next step or risk to validate (e.g., pilot in Tokyo first, or diligence a shortlist of acquisition targets) rather than treating the recommendation as final and certain.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 insight generation: candidate independently narrows to the craft segment, generates 3+ entry modes unprompted, and reasons through capital/control tradeoffs using the $30M constraint.
- 3/5: candidate reaches the segment-narrowing insight but needs a prompt to think beyond "just export it," or only generates 1–2 entry modes.
- 1/5: candidate evaluates the whole market as one block and recommends entering without addressing distribution/licensing barriers at all.
