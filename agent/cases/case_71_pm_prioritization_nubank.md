# Case File 71 — Nubank: Growing Revenue per Customer in Brazil (PM Prioritization Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM at Nubank Brazil. Next half you can build one of three things: letting customers pay with Pix in installments, a simple investment product for beginners, or better accounts for small business owners. Each has strong support internally. Your director wants a recommendation tied to the company's goal. How would you decide?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Choose the initiative that best grows revenue per customer, weighing revenue, risk, and reach.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal?" | Grow revenue per active customer in Brazil, while keeping credit losses under control. | On request -- key framing fact |
| 2 | "How much effort does each take?" | All three are roughly one half-year of work. | On request |
| 3 | "What's the expected impact?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about impact or revenue. | On request |
| 4 | "What risks come with each?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about risks. | On request |
| 5 | "How many active customers are in scope?" | About 90 million active customers in Brazil. | On request |
| 6 | "What do customers ask for?" | Many customers ask to split larger payments; beginners ask where to put savings safely; small business owners ask for better tools. | On request |
| 7 | "How do competitors compare?" | Several banks offer Pix installments; many apps offer investments; digital banks compete strongly for small businesses. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate which option to pick or how to weigh credit risk -- let them reason through it.

---

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Estimated Annual Impact**

| Initiative | Customers likely to use it | Extra revenue per user per year |
|---|---|---|
| Pix installments | 8% of active customers | about $40 (interest) |
| Beginner investments | 15% of active customers | about $6 (fees and balance margin) |
| Small business accounts | 3% of active customers | about $60 (fees and card spend) |

**Exhibit 2 — Risks**

| Initiative | Risk |
|---|---|
| Pix installments | It's a credit product: expected credit losses of about 25% of its interest revenue; higher if offered to riskier customers. |
| Beginner investments | Low credit risk; market downturns could cause complaints if expectations aren't managed. |
| Small business accounts | Competitive market; requires building new support and onboarding for businesses. |

---

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Anchor on the goal: revenue per active customer, with credit losses controlled.
2. Compute reach x revenue for each, net of risk.
3. Consider strategic factors (competition, new capabilities).
4. Recommend, with safeguards.

**Quantification a strong candidate does:**
- Pix installments: 90M x 8% = 7.2M users x $40 = $288M; minus ~25% losses = about $216M net.
- Investments: 90M x 15% = 13.5M x $6 = about $81M.
- Small business: 90M x 3% = 2.7M x $60 = about $162M, with more build and support cost.

**Correct core insight:** Pix installments has the largest net revenue (~$216M after expected losses) and matches what many customers already ask for, but it's a credit product, so the "losses under control" condition requires a careful rollout (limits based on credit history, starting with lower-risk customers). Small business is attractive but requires new capabilities. Investments is safest but smallest. A strong candidate picks Pix installments with explicit risk controls, or argues for small business with a strategic case.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Pix installments, rolled out first to customers with good credit history and with conservative limits.
- A plan to monitor losses by cohort before expanding.
- Metrics: revenue per active customer, installment adoption, loss rate by cohort.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: computes reach x revenue net of losses, picks with risk safeguards, and explains tradeoffs.
- 3/5: quantifies gross revenue but ignores losses or risk controls.
- 1/5: picks without numbers or ignores the credit risk.
