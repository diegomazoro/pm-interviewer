# Case File 1 — Peak Performance Sports (Profitability Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"Our client is Peak Performance Sports, a mid-sized chain of 40 sporting goods retail stores across the Midwest. The company has been profitable for years, but the CEO just got the results for the last fiscal year and profits dropped 30% versus the prior year, even though revenue was roughly flat. The CEO has hired us to figure out why profit fell and to recommend what to do about it. How would you approach this?"

**Objective (interviewer's private reference, not read aloud unless asked directly "what's the objective?"):** Diagnose the driver(s) of the profit decline and recommend actions to restore profitability.

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build (any logically equivalent structure is fine):**
1. Revenue drivers — price × volume, by segment/product/store.
2. Cost drivers — COGS/margin, and operating costs (labor, rent, SG&A).
3. External/market factors — competition, industry trends.
4. Internal/one-time factors — anything company changed.

**Correct core insight:** Revenue is flat, so the profit decline is almost entirely a gross-margin story, not a cost-control or revenue story. Margin fell from 44.9% to 38.0% (~$14M gross profit erosion) for two compounding reasons: (1) a mix shift toward lower-margin footwear and away from higher-margin apparel, and (2) increased discounting to match a new online competitor. Payroll cost also rose (partially offsetting the SG&A line's apparent decrease — note SG&A "decreased" overall due to other savings, but payroll hours specifically rose, which a sharp candidate should flag as a partial cost-side drag worth investigating separately, though it's the secondary factor).

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Re-balance product mix back toward higher-margin apparel (marketing push, buyer allocation, floor space reallocation) rather than just chasing footwear volume.
- Reassess promotional/discounting strategy — competing on price against an online-only player with lower cost structure is a losing game; consider service/experience differentiation or targeted (not blanket) promotions instead.
- Investigate the payroll hours increase — is it linked to service initiatives that could justify itself via higher apparel attach rates, or is it pure cost creep?
- Flag a risk/next step: quantify how much of the margin the company can realistically win back given the new competitive dynamic, rather than assuming a full return to last year's margin.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 insight generation: candidate isolates margin (not volume) as the story using the flat-revenue clue, and connects mix shift + discounting as the two compounding causes without being told to look at margin explicitly.
- 3/5: candidate finds one of the two causes but not both, or needs a nudge ("what about the P&L breakdown by category?") to get there.
- 1/5: candidate treats this as a revenue-decline case despite revenue being flat, or never requests Exhibit 1.
