# Case File 54 — Meta: Mid-Size Creators Posting Fewer Reels (PM Diagnostic Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a Product Manager on Instagram Reels, focused on creators. Over the last six weeks, the number of Reels posted by mid-size creators -- accounts with 10,000 to 100,000 followers -- has dropped 18%. Small and very large creators look normal. Creators are the supply side of Reels, so leadership is worried. How would you figure out what's going on?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Diagnose why mid-size creators are posting fewer Reels and recommend a response.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Did we change anything for creators recently?" | Six weeks ago the ranking system was updated to favor original content -- videos that aren't reposted from other apps or other accounts. | On request -- key fact |
| 2 | "Did monetization or payouts change?" | No changes to creator payouts or monetization programs. | On request -- rules out payouts |
| 3 | "Are mid-size creators leaving for other apps?" | Most are still active on Instagram (posting Stories and photos at normal rates); it's specifically Reels posting that dropped. | On request |
| 4 | "What happened to reach for mid-size creators?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about reach or views. | On request |
| 5 | "Is the drop concentrated in a type of creator or content?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked for a breakdown by content type or creator behavior. | On request |
| 6 | "What are creators saying?" | Reveal Exhibit 3 once asked about creator feedback or support tickets. | On request |
| 7 | "Why would small or large creators be unaffected?" | Large creators mostly produce original content with teams; small creators post less regularly overall and weren't heavy reposters. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not connect the originality ranking change, reposting behavior, and mid-size creators for the candidate -- let them find it.

## C. Exhibits / data

|---|---|
| Before ranking change | 8,400 |
| After ranking change | 6,700 |

**Exhibit 2 — Mid-Size Creators by Content Type**

|---|---|---|---|

- "My Reels suddenly get no views -- did I get shadowbanned?"

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Confirm the scope (Reels only, mid-size only) and rule out payouts or leaving the platform.
2. Look at what changed (ranking) and the reach data.
3. Segment by content type.
4. Decide whether this is a problem to fix or an intended effect, and how to manage it.

**Correct core insight:** The ranking change worked as intended -- reposted content lost reach (-70% views), original content gained slightly (+6%). Mid-size creators who mostly repost saw their reach collapse and posted 45% less, which drives the overall drop. Original creators are posting slightly more. The drop isn't necessarily bad for Reels quality, but it was handled badly: creators got no explanation and think they're "shadowbanned."

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Weighted change in posting: 0.35 x -45% + 0.65 x +2% = -15.75% + 1.3% = about -14% by creator count; the -18% in Reels is consistent if reposters were posting more often than average.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Don't roll back the ranking change; it improves content quality.
- Communicate clearly: tell creators what changed and why, show them when content is identified as reposted, and give guidance on original content.
- Help reposters become originators: tools to remix with credit, or templates and editing features.
- Track: original Reels posted, viewer satisfaction, creator retention, share of supply from original content.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 insight generation: connects the ranking change to reposters, segments the data, judges the drop as partly intended, and focuses the fix on communication and helping creators adapt.
- 3/5: finds the ranking change caused it but recommends rolling back, or doesn't segment.
- 1/5: assumes creators are leaving for competitors or payouts changed without checking.
