# Case File 73 — Nubank: Launching a Mobile Phone Plan in Mexico? (PM Strategy & Growth Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Nubank Mexico's strategy team. Leadership is debating whether to launch a mobile phone plan -- prepaid data and calls -- sold inside the Nu app, using another carrier's network. Some see it as a way to bring in new customers and engage existing ones; others see it as a distraction from banking. They want your recommendation. How would you approach this?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Recommend whether Nubank should launch a mobile plan in Mexico, based on strategic value, economics, and fit with the banking business.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal?" | Grow and engage customers in Mexico, especially to become their main financial app. | On request |
| 2 | "What are the economics of a mobile plan?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about economics or margins. | On request |
| 3 | "What's the strategic value -- does it help banking?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about effects on banking, engagement, or acquisition. | On request |
| 4 | "Who are the competitors?" | Large carriers dominate; several smaller brands sell plans on other carriers' networks with thin margins. | On request |
| 5 | "What do customers pay for mobile today?" | Most prepaid users spend about $10 a month, topping up in convenience stores. | On request |
| 6 | "What would it take to launch?" | A partnership with a carrier for network access, plus customer support for mobile issues -- a new capability for Nubank. | On request |
| 7 | "Is there a data angle?" | Mobile top-up and payment history could help assess creditworthiness for customers with little credit history -- subject to consent and regulation. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate whether to launch -- let them weigh thin standalone economics against strategic value.

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Mobile Plan Economics (per subscriber per year)**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Revenue | about $120 |
| Network and operating costs | about $105 |
| Margin | about $15 |
| Customer acquisition cost for a mobile brand (outside Nu's base) | about $25 |

**Exhibit 2 — Effects Seen in a Similar Program in Another Market**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Existing customers who signed up for the mobile plan | 6% |
| Of mobile subscribers, share who then made Nu their main account (salary or most spending) | 30% |
| Same metric among similar customers without the mobile plan | 12% |
| Annual banking revenue from a main-account customer vs. other customers | $90 vs. $25 |
| New customers who joined Nu because of the mobile plan | 15% of mobile subscribers |

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Clarify the goal (become the main financial app).
2. Evaluate standalone economics of the mobile plan.
3. Evaluate strategic value: effects on banking (main-account conversion, acquisition, data).
4. Weigh risks (new capability, distraction, margins).
5. Recommend with a test plan and metrics.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Standalone: about $15 margin per subscriber a year; with $25 acquisition cost outside the base, it takes almost two years to pay back -- a weak business on its own.
- Banking effect: 30% vs. 12% main-account conversion -> 18 extra points x ($90 - $25) = about $12 extra banking revenue per mobile subscriber a year -- similar to the mobile margin itself.
- Total value per subscriber roughly doubles when the banking effect is included, and acquisition within the existing base is nearly free.

**Correct core insight:** As a standalone business, a mobile plan is thin and not worth it. Its value is as a banking tool: it increases main-account conversion and brings new customers, roughly doubling the value per subscriber, and payment data may help credit decisions for thin-file customers. The decision depends on whether Nubank can run it without heavy new costs (support, partnership) -- so it should be tested on a limited scale.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Launch a limited pilot to existing customers (no acquisition cost), sold in the app with simple plans.
- Measure the banking effect (main-account conversion, engagement) vs. a matched control, not just mobile profit.
- Keep operating costs low through the carrier partnership; avoid big investment until the pilot proves the banking effect.
- Metrics: mobile adoption, main-account conversion, new customers from mobile, support costs.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: shows the standalone economics are thin, quantifies the banking effect, recognizes it as a strategic tool, and recommends a measured pilot.
- 3/5: recognizes strategic value but doesn't quantify, or only evaluates standalone economics.
- 1/5: launches because "customers love bundles," or rejects it purely on thin margins.
