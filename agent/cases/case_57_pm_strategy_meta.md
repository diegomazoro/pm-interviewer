# Case File 57 — Meta: An Ad-Free Instagram Subscription? (PM Strategy & Growth Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Instagram's monetization team. Instagram makes almost all its money from ads. Some leaders want to offer a paid, ad-free subscription worldwide, to diversify revenue and give users a choice. Others think it would lose money because ads earn more than people would pay. Leadership wants your recommendation. How would you approach this?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Recommend whether and where Instagram should offer an ad-free subscription, using ad revenue per user, willingness to pay, and strategic considerations.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal?" | Diversify revenue and offer user choice without reducing total revenue. In some regions, regulation also pushes toward offering a non-ad option. | On request |
| 2 | "How much does Instagram earn per user from ads?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about ad revenue per user or by region. | On request |
| 3 | "How many people would pay?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about willingness to pay or uptake. | On request |
| 4 | "Who would subscribe -- heavy or light users?" | Mostly heavy users, who also see the most ads and generate the most ad revenue. | On request -- key fact |
| 5 | "What would it cost to run?" | App store fees can take a large share of subscription revenue on mobile; web billing avoids most of it. | On request |
| 6 | "Are there regulatory considerations?" | In some regions, regulators expect users to be offered an option that doesn't use their personal data for ads. | On request |
| 7 | "What about advertisers?" | Advertisers worry about losing access to engaged users if many subscribe. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate whether to launch, where, or at what price -- let them do the math and weigh the strategy.

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Annual Ad Revenue per User (illustrative)**

| Region | Ad revenue per average user per year | Ad revenue per heavy user per year |
|---|---|---|
| US and Canada | $60 | $150 |
| Europe | $20 | $50 |
| Latin America | $5 | $12 |
| Asia-Pacific | $5 | $12 |

**Exhibit 2 — Subscription Test Assumptions**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Proposed price | $8 per month ($96 a year) |
| Expected uptake | 1-3% of users, mostly heavy users |
| App store fee on mobile subscriptions | up to 30% |

---

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Clarify goals (diversification, choice, regulation) and the constraint (don't reduce revenue).
2. Compare subscription revenue per subscriber to the ad revenue lost per subscriber -- by region and by user type.
3. Consider who subscribes (heavy users) and channel costs (app store fees).
4. Recommend where and how, with strategic and regulatory context.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Net subscription revenue: $96 a year, minus up to 30% app store fee -> about $67 on mobile, about $96 on web.
- US heavy user: loses $150 of ads vs. gains $67-96 -> loses money.
- Europe heavy user: loses $50 vs. gains $67-96 -> makes money.
- LatAm/APAC heavy user: loses $12 vs. gains $67-96 -> makes money per subscriber, but uptake at $8 may be very low given local incomes.

**Correct core insight:** Whether ad-free makes money depends on where. Subscribers are heavy users, so the comparison must use heavy-user ad revenue, not averages. In the US, ad-free loses money at $8; in Europe it gains, and regulation adds a reason to offer it there. In lower-ARPU regions it gains per subscriber but price must be adapted. A single worldwide price is the wrong frame.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Don't launch one worldwide price.
- Offer it first where it's revenue-positive and/or required (e.g. Europe), with web billing where possible.
- In the US, either don't offer it or price it above heavy-user ad revenue.
- Test regional pricing in lower-ARPU markets.
- Metrics: total revenue per subscriber vs. ad revenue lost, uptake, advertiser reach.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: uses heavy-user ad revenue, accounts for app store fees, compares by region, and recommends a region-specific approach with regulation in mind.
- 3/5: compares price to average ad revenue but misses the heavy-user effect or fees.
- 1/5: recommends a worldwide launch for diversification, or rejects it without numbers.
