# Case File 39 — Mercado Libre: Where to Invest for Growth in Mexico (PM Prioritization Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM at Mercado Libre Mexico. Next half, you can fund one of three big initiatives: same-day delivery in Mexico City, an advertising tool that lets small sellers promote their products, or working-capital loans for sellers through Mercado Pago. Each team has a strong pitch. Your country manager wants a recommendation. How would you decide?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Choose the initiative that best serves the stated goal using a consistent framework and quantified impact.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal for Mexico this year?" | Grow GMV -- total value of goods sold -- profitably. Initiatives that lose money are acceptable only if they pay back within two years. | On request -- key framing fact |
| 2 | "What does each cost?" | Same-day delivery: about $60M a year in logistics investment. Seller ads tool: about $5M to build. Seller loans: about $8M to build, plus capital lent (recovered as loans are repaid). | On request |
| 3 | "What's the expected impact of each?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about impact. | On request |
| 4 | "How confident are those estimates?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about confidence, evidence, or risks. | On request |
| 5 | "What does Mercado Libre earn on GMV?" | About 15% of GMV as revenue on marketplace sales; ads revenue is extra. | On request |
| 6 | "What's Mexico's GMV today?" | About $20B a year. | On request |
| 7 | "What do competitors do?" | One major competitor offers same-day delivery in Mexico City for its own products. Other marketplaces offer seller ads. Few offer seller loans. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate which initiative to choose or how to weigh confidence -- let them build the framework and quantify.

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Estimated Annual Impact**

| Initiative | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Same-day delivery in Mexico City | +$800M GMV (more frequent purchases from existing buyers in the city) |
| Seller ads tool | +$300M GMV from better product discovery, plus about $90M in ads revenue |
| Seller working-capital loans | +$1.2B GMV (sellers with loans stock more inventory and sell more); loan interest about $40M net of defaults |

**Exhibit 2 — Evidence Behind Each Estimate**

| Initiative | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Same-day delivery | Based on Brazil, where same-day delivery in São Paulo raised purchase frequency; Mexico City logistics are harder. |
| Seller ads tool | Based on a 3-month pilot with 2,000 sellers in Mexico. |
| Seller loans | Based on a small pilot with 500 sellers; defaults were low, but the pilot ran during a strong economy and only included top-rated sellers. |

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Anchor on the goal (profitable GMV growth, two-year payback).
2. Compare each initiative on GMV, revenue, cost, payback, and confidence.
3. Adjust for risk and evidence quality.
4. Recommend, with a way to reduce risk on the choice.

**Quantification a strong candidate does:**
- Same-day: $800M x 15% = $120M revenue vs. $60M a year cost -> roughly $60M net a year; pays back, but depends on harder logistics.
- Ads: $300M x 15% = $45M + $90M ads = $135M revenue for $5M -> best return on cost, strong evidence.
- Loans: $1.2B x 15% = $180M + $40M interest = $220M revenue for $8M plus capital -> biggest upside, weakest evidence (small, biased pilot; default risk).

**Correct core insight:** Loans have the biggest headline number, but the evidence is the weakest -- 500 top-rated sellers in a good economy won't predict defaults across all sellers. The ads tool has a very strong return on a small cost and the best evidence. A strong answer either picks ads (high confidence, high return) or picks loans with an explicit plan to expand gradually and protect against defaults -- and explains the risk tradeoff either way. Same-day delivery is the most expensive and least proven in Mexico City.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- A clear pick with reasoning on return and confidence.
- A risk plan: e.g. if ads is chosen, a staged loans pilot to more seller types; if loans is chosen, gradual expansion with default limits.
- Metrics: GMV impact, revenue, payback; default rate for loans; ads adoption and seller ROI.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: anchors on the goal, quantifies revenue vs. cost for all three, discounts for evidence quality, and recommends with a risk plan.
- 3/5: quantifies GMV but ignores cost or confidence.
- 1/5: picks the largest GMV number or same-day delivery "because customers love speed," without analysis.
