# Case File 51 — Google: Docs Priorities for Winning Enterprise Switchers (PM Prioritization Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Google Docs. Next half you can invest heavily in one of three areas: better offline editing, AI summaries of what changed in a document, or much better fidelity when importing and exporting Word files. The AI option has a lot of internal excitement. Your director wants a recommendation tied to the business. How would you decide?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Choose the investment that best serves the business goal, using data on what blocks enterprise wins.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the business goal?" | Win large enterprises that currently use a competing office suite, and get them to fully migrate. | On request -- key framing fact |
| 2 | "How much effort does each take?" | All three are roughly one half-year for the team. | On request |
| 3 | "What blocks enterprises from switching or completing migration?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about migration blockers or lost deals. | On request |
| 4 | "How do current users feel about each area?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about user demand or satisfaction. | On request |
| 5 | "How big are these enterprise deals?" | Large migrations are typically worth several million dollars a year each. | On request |
| 6 | "Do competitors already have AI summaries?" | Yes -- the main competitor recently launched one, and it gets attention in demos. | On request |
| 7 | "What happens when a migration stalls?" | Companies keep both suites, pay for both, and often eventually drop Google at renewal. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate which investment to choose -- let them weigh the data against the excitement around AI.

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Enterprise Migration Outcomes (last 12 months)**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Enterprise migrations started | 300 |
| Migrations stalled or reversed | 90 |
| Of stalled/reversed, main reason "Word documents break when imported/exported" | 40% |
| Of stalled/reversed, main reason "lack of offline editing" | 12% |
| Of stalled/reversed, main reason "missing AI features" | 5% |

|---|---|---|

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Anchor on the goal: enterprise migration wins.
2. Find what blocks migrations, using outcome data rather than excitement or surveys.
3. Quantify the revenue impact of each option.
4. Recommend and address the competitive pressure on AI.

**Quantification a strong candidate does:**
- Stalled/reversed migrations: 90 x $3M = $270M a year at stake.
- Word fidelity: 40% x 90 = 36 migrations x $3M = about $108M a year.
- Offline: 12% x 90 = about 11 migrations = about $32M.
- AI summaries: 5% x 90 = about 4-5 migrations = about $14M.

**Correct core insight:** AI summaries generate excitement and demo appeal, but Word fidelity is what actually breaks enterprise migrations -- about eight times more value than AI by this measure, with the lowest satisfaction score. The goal-driven choice is fidelity. A strong candidate still acknowledges competitive pressure on AI and suggests a lighter response (e.g. a smaller AI feature, or using existing models) without the full investment.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Invest in Word import/export fidelity, prioritizing the document types that break most in migrations.
- A smaller-scope plan for AI to stay competitive in demos.
- Metrics: migration completion rate, stalled migrations citing fidelity, fidelity test pass rate.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: anchors on migration wins, quantifies all three from the outcome data, resists the AI excitement with data, and handles the competitive angle.
- 3/5: picks fidelity without quantification, or picks AI with a reasonable but unquantified argument.
- 1/5: picks AI because it's exciting or most requested.
