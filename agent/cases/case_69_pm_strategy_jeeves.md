# Case File 69 — Jeeves: Entering Brazil (PM Strategy & Growth Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Jeeves' strategy team. Jeeves is growing well in Mexico and Colombia, and leadership wants to enter Brazil, the largest economy in Latin America. Brazil is very attractive, but also different: instant payments are extremely popular, and there are strong local competitors. Leadership wants your recommendation on whether and how to enter. How would you approach it?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Recommend whether and how Jeeves should enter Brazil, adapting its offering to local payment habits and competition.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the goal?" | Become a leading spend-management platform in Latin America within five years. | On request |
| 2 | "How do Brazilian businesses pay?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about payment habits. | On request |
| 3 | "Who are the competitors?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about competition. | On request |
| 4 | "What's needed to operate?" | Issuing cards and offering payments requires local partnerships or licenses; Jeeves would need a local partner bank to start, which takes about 6 months. | On request |
| 5 | "How big is the market?" | About 2 million SMBs and startups in the target segment, more than Mexico and Colombia combined. | On request |
| 6 | "What does Jeeves do well?" | Multi-currency spending (useful for companies paying foreign suppliers or software), fast onboarding, and a strong spend-management software layer. | On request |
| 7 | "What's Jeeves' model in Mexico?" | Card-first: most revenue is interchange on card spend; spend management software is the hook. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate whether to enter, which segment to target, or how to adapt the product -- let them reason through it.

---

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — How Brazilian SMBs Pay Business Expenses**

| Method | Share of business payments (by value) |
|---|---|
| Instant payments (Pix) | 55% |
| Bank slips (boleto) | 20% |
| Cards | 15% |
| Other | 10% |

Pix transfers are free or very cheap for businesses, so card interchange revenue is a smaller opportunity than in Mexico.

**Exhibit 2 — Competitive Landscape**

| Competitor type | Strength |
|---|---|
| Large digital banks with business accounts | Free Pix, cheap accounts, huge customer base, basic cards |
| Local spend-management startups | Corporate cards and expense software, well funded |
| Traditional banks | Relationships with established companies |

Gap: few competitors handle international spending (foreign suppliers, software in dollars) well.

---

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Assess attractiveness: market size and the goal.
2. Understand local behavior: Pix dominates; cards are a small share.
3. Assess competition and find Jeeves' right to win.
4. Adapt the business model and choose an entry segment.
5. Recommend, with entry plan and milestones.

**Correct core insight:** Brazil is big and strategically important, but copying the card-first model would fail: cards are only 15% of business payments, and free Pix undercuts interchange revenue. Local digital banks win on basic accounts, and local spend-management startups already offer corporate cards. Jeeves' differentiation is international spending -- multi-currency and paying foreign suppliers and software -- where competitors are weak. Enter with a focused segment (e.g. startups and tech-enabled SMBs with significant international spend), and integrate Pix into spend management rather than competing with it.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Card share is 15% vs. a card-first model built for markets where cards matter more -- the interchange pool is proportionally smaller.
- The target segment can be sized from the 2M SMBs by estimating the share with international spend.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Enter, but not card-first: lead with international spend and spend management, with Pix payments integrated (revenue from software subscriptions and FX, not just interchange).
- Target startups and SMBs with international spend first.
- Partner with a local bank to launch quickly; build local compliance.
- Metrics: customers in the target segment, revenue per customer by source (FX, subscription, interchange), share of spend managed.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: recognizes Pix changes the economics, finds the international-spend gap, adapts the business model, and picks a focused entry segment.
- 3/5: recommends entering with some adaptation but doesn't find a clear differentiation.
- 1/5: recommends entering with the same card-first model because the market is big.
