# Case File 31 — Revolut: Three Stakeholders, One Team (PM Prioritization Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM at Revolut leading a team in the retail app. For next quarter, three projects are competing for your team: leadership wants to expand the crypto trading features, the support team wants you to fix cards being wrongly declined at merchants, and the growth team wants a tool that makes it easy to switch your salary deposit to Revolut. You can realistically do one well. How would you decide?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Choose one project based on the company goal and data, and handle the stakeholder pressure with a clear rationale.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the company's main goal this year?" | Grow the number of customers who use Revolut as their main bank -- defined as receiving their salary into Revolut. | On request -- key framing fact |
| 2 | "Why do primary-bank customers matter?" | They generate about five times more revenue per customer than other customers and churn far less. | On request |
| 3 | "How much effort is each project?" | All three are roughly one quarter of work for the team. | On request |
| 4 | "What's the expected impact of the crypto expansion?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about crypto data. | On request |
| 5 | "How big is the declined-card problem?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about declines. | On request |
| 6 | "Why don't customers move their salary to Revolut?" | Reveal Exhibit 3 once asked about salary switching or primary-bank adoption. | On request |
| 7 | "How strongly does leadership feel about crypto?" | The CEO mentioned crypto in the last all-hands as a key area, but the yearly goal set by leadership is still primary-bank customers. | On request |
| 8 | "How many active customers are we talking about in total?" | About 20 million monthly active customers in the markets in scope; about 25% already receive their salary into Revolut. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate which project to choose or how to handle the CEO's interest in crypto -- they should reason it through and explain the tradeoff.

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Crypto Expansion**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Expected additional trading revenue in year one | about €40M |
| Share of active crypto users who already receive their salary into Revolut | 75% |

**Exhibit 2 — Card Declines**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Card transactions declined | 4% |
| Of declines, share that are wrongful (blocked by the fraud system but legitimate) | 30% |
| Customers who experience a wrongful decline each month | about 1.5 million |
| Likelihood of stopping card use within 30 days after a wrongful decline vs. no decline | 2x higher |
| Share of support tickets about declines | 22% |

**Exhibit 3 — Salary Switching**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Customers who say they'd consider moving their salary to Revolut | 40% of non-salaried customers |
| Top reason they haven't: "too much hassle to change details with my employer" | 60% |
| Expected effect of a switching tool (based on a similar tool at another bank) | 8% of non-salaried customers switch their salary within a year |
| Revenue per primary-bank customer vs. others | about €150 vs. €30 per year |

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Anchor on the yearly goal (primary-bank customers).
2. Evaluate each project's contribution to that goal, plus revenue, reach, and confidence.
3. Separate stakeholder enthusiasm from impact on the goal.
4. Make a call and plan how to handle the projects not chosen.

**Quantification a strong candidate does:**
- Salary switching: 20M x 75% non-salaried = 15M x 8% = 1.2M new primary customers x (€150 - €30) = about €144M of extra revenue a year once switched.
- Crypto: about €40M, but 75% of crypto users are already primary -- little impact on the goal.
- Declines: 1.5M customers a month affected, 2x churn risk, 22% of tickets -- real, but mostly protects existing usage rather than creating new primary customers; harder to quantify.

**Correct core insight:** The salary-switching tool maps directly to the company goal and has the largest quantified impact. Crypto has executive attention and visible revenue but mostly serves customers who are already primary. Wrongful declines are a serious quality problem (and the case for doing it soon is strong), but the goal-driven pick is salary switching. The strongest answers also suggest a way to address declines without the full team -- e.g. tuning the fraud thresholds, which may be a smaller fix.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Salary switching tool this quarter, tied to the goal and the numbers.
- A plan for declines (smaller fix, or next quarter) given the support and churn cost.
- A clear message to leadership on crypto: why it's deferred, and what would change the decision.
- Metrics: salary switches per month, primary-bank customer count, wrongful decline rate.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: anchors on the goal, quantifies at least two options, handles the CEO pressure with data, and has a plan for the other two.
- 3/5: picks a defensible option but doesn't quantify or ignores stakeholder management.
- 1/5: picks crypto because leadership wants it, or declines because "it's the top complaint," without connecting to the goal.
