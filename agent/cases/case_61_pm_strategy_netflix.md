# Case File 61 — Netflix: Bidding for Live Soccer Rights in Latin America (PM Strategy & Growth Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Netflix's strategy team for Latin America. The streaming rights to a major regional soccer league are up for auction. Some leaders think live soccer could bring in millions of new members and cut cancellations; others think sports rights are too expensive and not what Netflix is built for. Leadership wants your recommendation on whether to bid, and how much it's worth. How would you approach this?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Estimate the value of the rights to Netflix and recommend whether and how to bid.

---

## B. Clarifying-question answer key

| # | Likely question | Answer | Reveal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's the expected price?" | Bids are expected around $300M a year for a 4-year deal. | On request |
| 2 | "What would the rights include?" | Streaming rights to all league matches in Latin America, about 300 matches a year, with a season of 9 months. | On request |
| 3 | "How many new members might it bring?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about acquisition or audience. | On request |
| 4 | "Would it reduce cancellations?" | Reveal Exhibit 1 once asked about cancellations or retention (if not already shown). | On request |
| 5 | "What do members pay and what's the ad potential?" | Reveal Exhibit 2 once asked about revenue per member or advertising. | On request |
| 6 | "Does Netflix have experience with live sports?" | Netflix has streamed a few large live events, but never a full season of weekly matches; it would need production partners and stronger live-streaming capacity. | On request |
| 7 | "Who else is bidding?" | A sports-focused streaming service and a TV broadcaster that has held the rights for years. | On request |

**Guardrail reminder:** Do not tell the candidate whether to bid or what the maximum bid should be -- let them build the value estimate.

## C. Exhibits / data

**Exhibit 1 — Audience Estimates**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Fans who currently watch the league regularly | 40M |
| Estimated new Netflix members from the league | 2.5M in year one |
| Expected behavior of sports-driven members | Many cancel when the season ends: about 40% cancel in the off-season months |
| Estimated reduction in cancellations among existing members who are fans | 0.3 points of monthly churn among about 8M existing fan members |

**Exhibit 2 — Revenue Inputs**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Average revenue per member in Latin America | about $8 per month |
| Advertising revenue from live matches (estimate) | about $60M a year |
| Production and live-streaming costs | about $40M a year |

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Estimate revenue: new members (adjusted for seasonal cancellation), retention of existing members, advertising.
2. Subtract costs: rights fee, production.
3. Consider strategic value and risks (capability, competition, seasonality).
4. Recommend whether to bid and a maximum price.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- New members: 2.5M x $8 x 12 = $240M if all stayed; with about 40% leaving for the 3 off-season months, roughly 2.5M x $8 x (9 + 0.6 x 3) = 2.5M x $8 x 10.8 = about $216M.
- Retention: 8M existing fans x 0.3 points = about 24,000 fewer cancellations a month. Saved members accumulate to about 288,000 by year end (about 144,000 on average) -> 144,000 x $8 x 12 = about $14M a year.
- Advertising: $60M.
- Total value: about $216M + $14M + $60M = about $290M; minus $40M production = about $250M vs. a $300M rights fee -> roughly $50M short each year at the expected price.

**Correct core insight:** At the expected price, the rights roughly break even or lose money on direct economics -- the deal only works if Netflix values strategic benefits (brand, engagement, ad business growth) or can bid lower. The seasonality of sports-driven members is a key drag. Netflix should not win at any price; it should set a disciplined maximum bid below its estimated value, or consider a smaller package (e.g. top matches only).

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- A value estimate with assumptions, and a maximum bid (around $250M or below, depending on assumptions).
- Consider alternatives: a smaller package of marquee matches, or a sublicensing partnership.
- Plans to reduce off-season cancellations (bundle with other content, annual plans).
- Risks: live-streaming capability, competitors bidding higher.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: builds a structured value estimate including seasonal churn, retention and ads, compares to cost, and recommends a disciplined bid or alternative.
- 3/5: estimates revenue but ignores seasonality or costs.
- 1/5: recommends bidding because soccer is popular, without numbers.
