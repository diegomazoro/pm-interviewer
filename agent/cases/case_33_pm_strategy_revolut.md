# Case File 33 — Revolut: Should Revolut Offer Mortgages? (PM Strategy & Growth Case)

## A. Case prompt (read aloud to candidate at the start — nothing else revealed yet)

"You're a PM on Revolut's strategy team. In one large European market where Revolut has a full banking license, leadership is debating whether to launch mortgages. Mortgages are the biggest financial product most people ever take, and banks use them to win lifelong customers. But it's a very different business from what Revolut does today. They've asked you for a recommendation. How would you approach it?"

**Objective (private reference, reveal only if asked directly):** Recommend whether and how Revolut should enter mortgages in this market, considering its customer base, economics, capital, and alternatives like partnering.

## D. Model answer / "what good looks like"

**Framework a strong candidate should build:**
1. Clarify the goal (main-bank relationships over revenue).
2. Size the realistic demand from Revolut's own customers, not the whole market.
3. Compare lending directly vs. partnering on economics, capital, and risk.
4. Weigh strategic value (primary-bank relationship) and capability gaps.
5. Recommend a path with milestones.

**Quantitative reasoning a strong candidate shows:**
- Near-term demand: 3.0M x 6% x 35% = 63,000 customers over 2 years -- about 31,500 a year at most, before conversion losses.
- Direct lending: if Revolut wins, say, 10,000 mortgages a year: €20M of margin per year (growing as the book builds) but €100M of capital a year and €60M upfront.
- Partner model: 10,000 x €1,500 = €15M a year, no capital, little setup.
- Note: only 22% are salaried at Revolut -- many customers don't yet trust Revolut with their main financial life, which matters for a mortgage.

**Correct core insight:** Revolut's base is young and mostly not yet primary-banking with it, so near-term mortgage demand is modest. Lending directly means a big upfront cost and capital tied up for decades for a small initial volume. Partnering captures most of the strategic value (being where customers start their home journey) at a fraction of the risk, and builds the data to decide later whether to lend directly.

**A strong final recommendation includes:**
- Start with a partner model: a great in-app home-buying journey (affordability, savings for deposit, mortgage offers from a partner).
- Use it to build the primary-bank relationship and learn demand; set criteria for moving to direct lending later (volume, approval rates, capital position).
- Metrics: mortgages originated through the app, salary switches among home buyers, partner approval rates.
- Risks: partner experience quality reflects on Revolut; regulatory expectations.

**Scoring anchors:**
- 5/5 business sense: sizes realistic demand from the customer base, compares direct vs. partner with capital in mind, and recommends a staged path tied to the main-bank goal.
- 3/5: reasonable recommendation but uses total market size, or ignores capital.
- 1/5: recommends launching full mortgage lending because the market is big.
